# What is functional harmony?

**Functional harmony** is the system in Western tonal music where chords don't just exist in isolation—they serve specific **roles (functions)** that create a sense of tension, direction, and resolution toward a central key center (the tonic).

Rather than treating every chord as an independent sound, functional harmony groups all chords in a key into **three main functional categories**:

---

### The Three Harmonic Functions

```
[ Tonic ]  <========  [ Dominant ]  <========  [ Subdominant ]
(Home / Rest)          (Peak Tension)          (Departure / Prep)

```

1. **Tonic (T) — Home / Resolution**
* **Role:** Represents rest, stability, and completion. It is the "ground zero" of a key where harmonic tension resolves.
* **Primary Chords:** **$\text{I}$** (or $\text{i}$ in minor).
* **Secondary Substitutes:** **$\text{vi}$** and **$\text{iii}$**.


2. **Subdominant / Predominant (S or PD) — Departure / Preparation**
* **Role:** Moves away from the tonic to build momentum. It sets up and prepares the strong pull of the dominant chord.
* **Primary Chords:** **$\text{IV}$** (or $\text{iv}$) and **$\text{ii}$** (or $\text{ii}^\circ$).


3. **Dominant (D) — Tension / Direction**
* **Role:** Contains maximum harmonic tension, creating an irresistible urge to resolve back to the Tonic.
* **Primary Chords:** **$\text{V}$** (or $\text{V}^7$) and **$\text{vii}^\circ$**.
* **Why it works:** The $\text{V}^7$ chord contains a **tritone** (the interval between the 3rd and 7th of the chord) that pulls strongly toward the 1st and 3rd scale degrees of the Tonic chord.



---

### The Fundamental Progression: $\text{ii} - \text{V} - \text{I}$

The classic $\text{ii} - \text{V} - \text{I}$ progression (or $\text{IV} - \text{V} - \text{I}$ in classical music) is the quintessential example of functional harmony in action:

* **$\text{Dm}^7$ ($\text{ii}$):** **Subdominant** — Prepares the transition away from home.
* **$\text{G}^7$ ($\text{V}^7$):** **Dominant** — Builds maximum tension.
* **$\text{Cmaj}^7$ ($\text{I}$):** **Tonic** — Resolves the tension back home.

---

### Functional vs. Non-Functional Harmony

* **Functional Harmony:** Used in Baroque, Classical, Romantic, Pop, and traditional Jazz. Chords drive toward a target goal (cadence).
* **Non-Functional Harmony:** Used in Impressionism (Debussy), Modal Jazz (Kind of Blue), and Atonal/Post-Tonal music (Pitch-Class theory). Chords are chosen for their unique color, texture, or voice-leading without needing to resolve to a home key.

### Key Information & Chapters

In *Improvising Jazz*, Coker addresses functional harmony as the core framework improvisers use to understand chord progressions and navigate key centers.

* **Chapter 8: Functional Harmony**
This entire chapter breaks down how chords function relative to key centers, focusing on how jazz musicians simplify complex lead sheets by recognizing functional movement rather than treating every chord as an isolated event.
* **Core Topics Covered:**
* **Roman Numeral Analysis:** Translating chord symbols into scale degree functions ($\text{I}, \text{ii}, \text{IV}, \text{V}^7$).
* **The $\text{ii}-\text{V}-\text{I}$ Progression:** Identifying subdominant-to-dominant-to-tonic resolutions as the primary functional engine in jazz standards.
* **Secondary Dominants:** Explaining chords that temporarily function as a $\text{V}^7$ moving to degrees other than the tonic (e.g., $\text{V}^7/\text{ii}$).
* **Turnarounds:** How functional progressions cycle back to the tonic or set up repeating choruses.
